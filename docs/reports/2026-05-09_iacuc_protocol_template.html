B.Project summary (lay summary)

This protocol describes the use of zebrafish (Danio rerio) embryos to study how transcription factors control kidney development. The kidney is one of the most complex organs in vertebrates, and understanding which genes are "master regulators" — those that direct other genes during organ formation — has implications for understanding congenital kidney disease in humans and for tissue engineering. Zebrafish are an ideal model because their embryos are transparent, develop externally and rapidly (kidney function begins by 48 hpf), are easy to manipulate genetically, and have well-conserved kidney development genes with humans.

We have identified several candidate transcription factors via single-cell RNA sequencing (a technique that profiles thousands of individual cells) of published datasets. Our top candidate is [hoxb8a OR mafba — to be specified per active experiment], which is expressed in pronephros progenitor cells but whose function has not been tested. We will use CRISPR-Cas9 to knock out this gene in zebrafish embryos, and again profile gene expression at single-cell resolution to determine if the knockout disrupts kidney development.

The experiment will use ~600 zebrafish embryos (loss <50% expected from injection), all younger than 120 hours post-fertilization. No adult fish will be sacrificed beyond standard breeding practices. Embryos are euthanized humanely at experimental endpoints (24, 48, 72 hpf) using approved methods.

The experimental knowledge generated will inform Phase II of the project (mouse extension) and contribute to the broader field of regenerative medicine.

C.Animal use justification

C.1 — Why use animals at all?

The pronephros (early kidney) is a tissue that develops in vivo through complex interactions between cell types and signaling gradients that cannot be replicated in cell culture. Single-cell RNA sequencing requires intact tissue dissociated at specific developmental timepoints, which can only be obtained from living embryos. In silico modeling and cell line work cannot substitute for in vivo functional analysis of transcription factor regulation during organogenesis.

C.2 — Why zebrafish specifically?

  • Transparent embryos allow real-time observation of organ formation without sacrifice.
  • External fertilization and rapid development (organogenesis at 24-72 hpf) match the experimental window precisely.
  • Single-cell genetics established: high-quality scRNA-seq atlases exist (Schoels 2021, Wagner 2018) for cross-validation.
  • CRISPR-Cas9 highly efficient in zebrafish embryos via 1-cell stage microinjection.
  • Pronephros conservation: zebrafish pronephros uses orthologous transcription factors as mammalian metanephros, making it a relevant model.
  • Lower regulatory burden on embryonic stages vs. mammalian models, supporting the "Reduction" principle of the 3Rs.
  • Faster turnaround (3-4 months vs. 12+ months for mouse work) reduces total animal use across the project.

C.3 — Why not in vitro / in silico alternatives?

  • scRNA-seq atlases of zebrafish pronephros exist (Schoels, Wagner) and have been used for our preliminary computational survey. The current protocol IS the wet-lab follow-up validation that those datasets cannot provide on their own.
  • Cell lines (e.g., human podocyte lines) lack the cell-cell interactions and morphogenetic context essential for transcription factor functional assessment.
  • Organoid systems (e.g., kidney organoids) are still in development and do not faithfully recapitulate full pronephros segmentation.
  • In silico predictions (the survey work) cannot resolve causality vs. correlation — only loss-of-function in vivo can.

C.4 — Why developmental stage 24-72 hpf?

Pronephros development in zebrafish: progenitor specification at ~14 hpf, tubule formation at 24-30 hpf, glomerular function at 40-48 hpf, fully mature pronephros at 72 hpf. The window 24-72 hpf captures specification through maturation, allowing comparison of effects at different developmental stages. All timepoints are within the <120 hpf range where embryos are not free-feeding and IACUC-exempt under most US guidelines.

Replacement

Where possible, computational pre-screening (already executed) reduced from 13 candidate TFs to 1 priority target via:

  • Cross-cohort validation with public Wagner GSE112294
  • Pleiotropy assessment via Wagner whole-embryo data

This represents a 13× reduction in candidate experiments before any animal use.

Reduction

n animals justified by power analysis (item 3 pre-resolve):

  • n = 200-300 inj/group × 4 groups = 600 embryos initial
  • Justified for 80%+ power at 30% effect size, 3% target cluster baseline
  • Minimum to support multiple comparisons + biological replicates
  • F0 crispants used INSTEAD of stable line generation, avoiding multiple breeding generations (saving ~80% of animal use vs. stable line approach)

Refinement

Procedures designed to minimize distress:

  • Microinjection at 1-cell stage (no anesthesia needed; zygote not yet differentiated)
  • Embryos maintained in standard E3 medium, 28.5°C
  • Humane endpoints clearly defined (see §H)
  • Euthanasia by tricaine (MS-222) overdose followed by ice-water immersion (AVMA-approved)
  • No survival surgery beyond microinjection
  • Daily welfare monitoring through scRNA-seq endpoint

E.2 — Statistical justification (from item 3 power analysis)

Power analysis (see power analysis visual report) demonstrates:

  • n=5,000-8,000 cells per scRNA-seq sample → 80%+ power for 30% relative effect at 3% target cluster baseline
  • 12 samples (4 groups × 3 stages) → ~60K-96K cells total
  • ~10-15 embryos per sample for tissue dissection (zebrafish pronephros yield ~500-1000 cells per embryo post-dissociation)
  • Multiple comparisons corrected via BH-FDR at α=0.05

Lower n would result in underpowered detection of biological effects, requiring re-experiment and ultimately MORE animal use. Higher n offers diminishing returns and is not justified.

E.3 — Replicates

Three biological replicates (3 independent parental crosses) for each treatment group, randomized within and between clutches to control for clutch-specific variation.

F.Experimental procedures

F.1 — Adult breeding (no experimental manipulation)

Adult AB strain (or [INSTITUTION COLONY STRAIN]) zebrafish are paired in standard breeding tanks the evening before injection. Embryos are collected within 30 min of fertilization for microinjection at the 1-cell stage. After breeding, adults return to home tanks under standard housing conditions per institutional zebrafish facility SOPs (~28.5°C, 14h light / 10h dark, monitored daily).

F.2 — Microinjection (1-cell stage embryos)

  • Reagents prepared:
    • CRISPR-Cas9 RNP: Cas9 protein (IDT Alt-R, 0.5 μg/μL) + sgRNA (IDT Alt-R, 1 μg/μL) pre-complexed at 37°C × 10 min
    • OR Splice-blocking morpholino (Gene Tools, 0.5-2 ng/embryo, dose-titrated in pilot)
    • Phenol red 0.05% as injection tracer
  • Injection volume: 1-2 nL per embryo, into the cell (yolk-cell interface) at 1-cell stage
  • Injection equipment: Eppendorf FemtoJet or equivalent; standard glass capillary needles
  • Throughput: ~50-100 embryos/hour; total injection time ~6-8 hours per session, distributed across 3 sessions (3 biological replicates)
  • Post-injection: embryos transferred to E3 medium in petri dishes (~50 embryos/dish) at 28.5°C incubator

F.3 — Embryo monitoring (24-72 hpf)

  • Daily observations: survival count, gross morphology (heart beat, body shape, edema), pigmentation
  • Pre-defined humane endpoints: see §H
  • Imaging (optional): live brightfield + fluorescence at 24, 48, 72 hpf for transgenic lines (if Tg(wt1a:GFP) or similar used)

F.4 — Genotyping (F0 crispants)

At each scRNA-seq endpoint, a subset of injected embryos (n=10/group) is genotyped via High-Resolution Melt Analysis (HRMA) on PCR amplicon spanning the sgRNA cut site. Embryos with editing efficiency <50% (estimated by HRMA peak shift) are excluded from downstream analysis. This is post-hoc selection, not an experimental treatment.

F.5 — Tissue dissection for scRNA-seq

  • Embryos (10-15 per sample) are anesthetized in 0.16 mg/mL tricaine (MS-222), then transferred to dissection buffer
  • Pronephros tissue is microdissected using fine forceps under stereomicroscope (Sagar lab protocol, Schoels 2021)
  • Pronephros tissue is enzymatically dissociated (collagenase + protease) to single-cell suspension
  • Filtration through 40 μm strainer; viability assessed via Trypan blue (>80% live for proceed)
  • Cells loaded onto 10x Genomics 3' v3.1 platform

F.6 — Tissue collection for WISH validation

Subset of embryos (n=10/group/stage) is fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for whole-mount in situ hybridization (WISH) with probes against wt1a, podxl, nphs1 (for podocyte markers) or pax2a, lhx1a (for progenitor markers, hoxb8a context).

G.Anesthesia and euthanasia

G.1 — Anesthesia

Embryos older than 24 hpf may be anesthetized for fine manipulation using tricaine methanesulfonate (MS-222) at 0.16 mg/mL in E3 medium. Recovery is achieved by transfer to fresh E3. Only used during tissue dissection at scRNA-seq endpoints; not used for routine handling.

G.2 — Euthanasia

All experimental embryos are euthanized at the scRNA-seq endpoint or at predetermined humane endpoints. AVMA-approved methods for zebrafish:

  • Primary method: tricaine overdose (≥250 mg/L MS-222 in E3) for ≥10 min
  • Confirmation: ice-water immersion (4°C) for ≥10 min after tricaine overdose
  • Disposal: as biological waste per institutional protocol; CRISPR-edited animals require recombinant DNA disposal protocol (see §I)

For embryos <24 hpf, primary method alone is sufficient given less developed nervous system. For embryos 24-120 hpf, both steps performed.

H.Humane endpoints

Embryos are monitored daily; embryos meeting ANY of the following criteria are euthanized humanely (see §G) before reaching the scheduled scRNA-seq timepoint:

  • No heartbeat at expected developmental stage (heartbeat begins ~24 hpf; absence at 36 hpf = endpoint)
  • Severe dysmorphology (body axis defects beyond mild bend, severe yolk extension defects, complete failure of pigmentation)
  • Severe edema (full-body) without recovery within 12 hours
  • Necrosis (>25% body coverage) or massive cell death visible
  • No response to touch at stages where motility is expected (touch response begins ~21 hpf)

Note: in CRISPR F0 crispant experiments, partial dysmorphology is expected as part of the phenotype and not all "abnormal" embryos require euthanasia. Trained personnel must distinguish between informative phenotype and severe distress.

Reporting

All early euthanasia events (departing from scheduled endpoints) are logged in a research log including date, embryo identifier, observation, and rationale. Reported to attending veterinarian within 24h if >10% of cohort affected.

I.Biosafety + recombinant DNA (IBC review required)

⚠️ IMPORTANT: CRISPR-Cas9 use constitutes recombinant DNA work. Institutional Biosafety Committee (IBC) approval is required SEPARATELY from IACUC. Both must be approved before experiment proceeds.

I.1 — Biosafety level

Biosafety Level 1 (BL1) for zebrafish CRISPR-Cas9 work in most US institutions. The Cas9 protein + synthetic sgRNA does not pose containment risk to humans or environment beyond standard zebrafish husbandry. [CONFIRM PER INSTITUTION POLICY]

I.2 — Containment

  • All injected embryos and progeny housed in dedicated CRISPR-edited animal area within the zebrafish facility
  • No release to non-CRISPR tanks; no mixing with wild-type breeding colonies until F1 (not part of this protocol)
  • Disposal: CRISPR-edited carcasses (post-euthanasia) treated as recombinant DNA biological waste per institutional SOP
  • Effluent from dissociation steps: contains lysed CRISPR-edited cells; treated as recombinant DNA waste

I.3 — Personal protective equipment

  • Laboratory coat, nitrile gloves, eye protection during all microinjection and dissection procedures
  • Standard zebrafish facility precautions (closed-toed shoes, no eating/drinking)

L.1 — Specific alternatives evaluated and rejected

  • Stable mutant line generation: rejected — requires 4-6 months and ~5× more animals; F0 crispants achieve same scientific goal with less animal use.
  • Bulk RNA-seq alone: rejected — does not provide cell-type-specific resolution needed to evaluate cluster-specific phenotype.
  • In vitro kidney organoids: rejected — current organoid systems do not faithfully recapitulate pronephros segmentation needed to test the hypothesis.
  • Mouse models: rejected for Phase I — higher animal welfare costs, slower turnaround. Mouse extension planned for Phase II based on this Phase I result.
