(1) Jagged2–Notch complex

A ligand–receptor trans-signaling pair, not a TF complex. The Jagged N-terminal MNNL/C2 + DSL domain engages the Ca²⁺-binding EGF repeats 8–12 of the Notch ectodomain on an adjacent cell → ADAM10/17 (S2) then γ-secretase (S3) cleavage → NICD release → RBPJ/CSL + MAML target activation. Zebrafish Jagged2 exists as two co-orthologs (jag2a, jag2b) from the teleost genome duplication; receptors notch1a/1b/2/3.
Honest caveat (drives the low subclaim confidence): the strongest human anterior-segment Notch genetics is JAG1–NOTCH2 (Alagille syndrome; posterior embryotoxon, Axenfeld-Rieger-like). JAG2 is more classically craniofacial/otic/limb/thymus. Pairing "Jagged2" with anterior-segment is plausible on periocular neural-crest grounds but is not the canonical axis — flag before any design use.

(2) Foxc1a–Pitx2 complex

A transcription-factor complex / regulatory antagonism, not receptor–ligand. FOXC1 (forkhead) and PITX2 (paired-like homeodomain) are the two dosage-sensitive Axenfeld-Rieger / anterior-segment-dysgenesis genes. In mammals PITX2 physically binds FOXC1 and represses its transcriptional activity (Berry et al., Hum Mol Genet 2006) — a direct protein–protein interaction with functional feedback. Zebrafish maps FOXC1 to co-orthologs foxc1a (dominant periocular) + foxc1b, with a single pitx2. The complex acts in periocular neural-crest-derived mesenchyme patterning the anterior segment.
